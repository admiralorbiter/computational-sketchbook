📖 Chapter 1: Meet Sarah

Meet Sarah, a college student who loves both coffee and donuts. She has a weekly budget of $20 to spend on these treats. Let's explore how she makes her choices!

💰 Chapter 2: The Budget Line

Sarah's budget creates a constraint on what she can afford. The budget line shows all possible combinations of coffee and donuts she can buy with her $20.

Try changing the prices and budget to see how the budget line shifts!

Budget Analysis

📊 Chapter 3: Sarah's Preferences

Sarah has preferences about how much she values coffee vs donuts. Indifference curves show combinations that give her the same level of happiness (utility).

Explore how different utility levels create different indifference curves!

Preference Analysis

🎯 Chapter 4: Finding the Best Choice

Sarah wants to maximize her happiness while staying within her budget. The optimal choice occurs where an indifference curve just touches the budget line.

This is called the "tangency condition" - where the slope of the indifference curve equals the slope of the budget line!

Optimal Choice Analysis

🔄 Chapter 5: How Changes Affect Choices

What happens when prices or income change? Let's explore how Sarah's optimal choice responds to different economic conditions.

This is called "comparative statics" - comparing different equilibrium situations!
