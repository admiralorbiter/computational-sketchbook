Board Brief-June 18, 2026
The Board Brief provides information about what took place during our board meetings. It provides details on Board discussions, actions, and items of interest to our community. Please check Board Briefs often to stay up to date on the important decisions being made in our district.
Preliminary Budget
The Board adopted a preliminary budget for 2026-27.  The preliminary operating budget was developed by beginning with the proposed final budget from the 2025-2026 fiscal year.
Career Ladder Update
Assistant Superintendent Stephanie Amaya, shared an update on Career Ladder. The Missouri Career Development and Teacher Excellence Plan, commonly known as the Career Ladder Program, is a voluntary performance-based compensation initiative established by state statute. It aims to provide salary supplements to teachers who undertake additional responsibilities and voluntary efforts beyond their contractual obligations. The program is designed to recognize and reward teacher excellence, thereby promoting higher retention rates and enhancing student outcomes.  There were over 8,367 hours reported by teachers supporting students beyond their regular assignments.
The Grandview C-4 School Board approved the implementation of this program on April 17, 2025 after a year-long planning process by District administration and a teacher committee.
Activities Director
At the June board meeting Dr. Rodrequez introduced the new Activities Director.  Jeremy Brown comes to us from Springfield Public School.  He has worn several hats in his career including: Principal, Assistant Principal, Athletic Director, Dean of Students, teacher, and coach.  Mr. Brown is currently working on his Doctorate in School Leadership.
Real World Learning Opportunities
The district is continuing its partnership with T&L Welding.  This partnership will allow students to participate in a welding program that gives students the opportunity to earn 200 hours of welding coursework!  Grandview is also partnering with Between Me 2 You.  Between Me 2 You provide healthcare training programs such as, CNA, Phlebotomy, and other approved pathways.

ITEM [2:2] b. Hop Skip Drive (ID: lg57UwReY7kWmhfAbnQJHg==)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[Background]:
Administration is requesting Board approval of a Transportation Coordination License and Services Agreement between the Grandview C-4 School District and HopSkipDrive, Inc. HopSkipDrive is a student transportation coordination provider that facilitates transportation services for students through a network of vetted drivers and transportation partners. The agreement provides the District with an additional transportation option to support student attendance, access to educational programs, McKinney-Vento services, foster care transportation needs, special program transportation, and other circumstances where traditional transportation options may not be available or practical.
The agreement will be effective from March 19, 2026, through March 19, 2027, with the option for annual renewal upon mutual agreement of both parties. Either party may terminate the agreement with thirty (30) days written notice. Under the agreement, HopSkipDrive will provide transportation coordination services through its platform and maintain required insurance coverage, including automobile liability, general liability, sexual misconduct and molestation coverage, and employer liability insurance. The agreement also includes FERPA compliance provisions, background check requirements for individuals providing services, and student data privacy protections.
Transportation services will be utilized on an as-needed basis. Costs are based on a fee structure that includes a base fare, mileage rate, and any applicable additional services. There is no minimum service commitment required by the District.

[Recommendation]:
Approve of the Transportation Coordination License and Services Agreement between Grandview C-4 School District and HopSkipDrive, Inc. to provide supplemental student transportation services as outlined in the agreement.

ITEM [2:4] d. Meal Service Prices (ID: zXJ8vplusYeEbEtjEysyfXmgQ==)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[Background]:
The Healthy, Hunger-Free Kids Act was signed into law by President Obama on December 13, 2010.  One of the provisions of the legislation mandates that school nutrition programs move towards charging paid meal category students at a price that is on average equal to the difference between free meal reimbursement and paid meal reimbursement.  Schools that charge less than this amount are required to gradually increase their prices over time until they meet the requirement. Attachment 1 shares historical Grandview meal prices.
We are recommending a 10-cent increase to all breakfast and lunch meal prices for the 2026-2027 school year, which would result in the following prices:
Elementary breakfast -- $2.35
Middle School breakfast -- $2.55
High School breakfast -- $2.60
Adult breakfast -- $3.25
Elementary lunch -- $3.10
Middle School lunch -- $3.20
High School lunch -- $3.35
Adult lunch -- $4.10
Reduced Breakfast $.30
Reduced Lunch $.40

[Recommendation]:
Recommend that the Board of Education act to approve a 10-cent increase to all breakfast and lunch meal prices, as outlined in background information, for the 2026-2027 school year.

ITEM [2:2] b. Safety Screenings (ID: 8qvegTUQLBslshwlNguHQzy2w==)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[Background]:
During the 2016-17 school year, the Grandview C-4 School District began conducting safety and security screenings at all secondary school sites.  The screenings began in response to incidents in our schools involving weapons, which compromised the safety of our students and staff.  Those incidents, along with ongoing national-level school safety concerns, continue to result in the need for increased levels of security measures in our schools. The screenings include the use of a metal detector wand and process that is similar to what is used at sporting event venues.  Our screening process is designed to minimize the amount of time students are out of the classroom and continue to be supported by our District safety committee.
Since they began, the screenings that have been conducted at all secondary schools have been successful in confiscating weapons and other unauthorized items that either should not be at school or should be announced to staff, such as prescription medication. This process has led to a decrease in the number of unauthorized items brought into the school overall.   The screening process continues to be improved and is supported by our District safety committee. In order to maintain an ongoing safe and secure environment for all students and staff, the district and site administration recommend the continuing practice of conducting security screenings throughout the school year at both the high school and middle school levels.
