## 1. Executive Summary & Regional Context

The three southern suburban school districts of Jackson County, Missouri—**Consolidated School District No. 4 (Grandview C-4)**, **Center School District No. 58 (Center 58)**, and **Consolidated School District No. 1 (Hickman Mills C-1)**—formed a collaborative microregional consortium to share CTE career pathways, dual-credit coursework, and Market Value Asset (MVA) opportunities under the Kauffman Foundation's Real World Learning initiative.

### Breakthrough Findings
1. **Identical Grant Arc & Synchronous Termination**: All three districts received nearly identical six-year funding allocations totaling **~$630k–$640k** across three synchronized agreements. Crucially, **all three grant commitments expired simultaneously on December 31, 2024**, with **$0 approved for future payment** on Kauffman's 2024 Form 990-PF.
2. **Refutation of Regional Crowding-Out**: In **none** of the three districts did Career and Technical Education spending decline following the introduction or expiration of philanthropic grants. Across the microregion, statutory career expenditures rose from **$3.71 Million (FY19)** to **$4.52 Million (FY24)**.
3. **Pervasive Local Absorption Capacity**: Propelled by sharp increases in Jackson County property valuations, assessed valuation grew between **+58.7% and +79.7%** across the three districts. Combined district reserve fund balances nearly tripled from **$44.0 Million in FY19** to **$126.3 Million in FY24**, providing extraordinary fiscal capacity to absorb operational costs.

## 4. Distinct Structural Archetypes in the Microregion

While all three districts demonstrate strong local absorption, their operational mechanisms differ substantially:

### 1. The High-Capacity In-House Academy Model (Hickman Mills C-1)
- **Scale**: Hickman Mills spends over **$3.38 Million annually** on CTE, representing **74.8%** of the entire microregion's career spending.
- **Independence from External Grants**: The $135k Kauffman grant represents only **3.99%** of Hickman's career budget. The district's career academy infrastructure at Ruskin High School was already heavily institutionalized prior to Kauffman's RWL initiative; the foundation dollars served as incremental enhancement rather than baseline support.
- **Fiscal Cushion**: Hickman's reserves expanded by **453.8%**, reaching $53.0 Million in FY24.

### 2. The Aggressive Growth & Consortium Sending Model (Center 58)
- **Expansion Rate**: Center expanded career education expenditures faster than any peer (+192.6%), surging from $228k to $668k.
- **Tuition Investment**: Center relies heavily on sending students to specialized Area Career Centers (Function 1921), increasing tuition payments from $138.7k in FY19 to **$391.0k in FY24**.
- **Local Multiplier**: In FY24, Center invested **$4.95 of local tax money for every $1.00 of Kauffman grant funding**.

### 3. The On-Campus Pathway & Corporate Sponsorship Model (Grandview C-4)
- **Internalization Focus**: Grandview deliberately held off-site Area Career Center tuition flat ($158k $\rightarrow$ $141k) while **doubling on-campus instruction ($170k $\rightarrow$ $333k)**.
- **Corporate Co-Investment**: Grandview offset the expiration of foundation grant money by securing **$125,000 from Honeywell FM&T / KCNSC** for its high school advanced manufacturing laboratory.

## 5. Regional Synthesis Verdict

$$\mathbf{Conclusion:\ Broad\ Structural\ Absorption\ Across\ the\ Microregion}$$

The multi-district empirical comparison across Grandview, Center, and Hickman Mills firmly answers the central inquiry:
- **No Evidence of Decay**: Across all three districts, career connected expenditures reached all-time highs in the final grant year (FY24).
- **No Evidence of Substitution / Crowding-Out**: In no district did outside grant checks displace local public funding.
- **The Dec 31, 2024 Transition**: Because all three districts substantially grew their statutory Career Education budgets and accumulated tens of millions in local reserve balances, the conclusion of direct Kauffman grant funding on December 31, 2024 represents a **managed transition to local sustainability**, rather than an operational cliff.
