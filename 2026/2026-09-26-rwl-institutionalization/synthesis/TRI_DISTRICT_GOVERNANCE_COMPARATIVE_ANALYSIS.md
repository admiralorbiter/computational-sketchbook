## Executive Summary

When the Ewing Marion Kauffman Foundation's Real World Learning (RWL) grant portfolio reached its uniform statutory expiration on **December 31, 2024**, each of the three contiguous South Kansas City public school districts faced the identical structural cliff: the cessation of philanthropic operating subsidies (\$628k to \$797k cumulative per district) with \$0 in approved future funding.

By triangulating DESE statutory finance panels, IRS 990-PF grant schedules, and a census of **1,501 official school board meetings** (yielding **732 deep-indexed agenda items** across focal transition sessions), this empirical investigation reveals **three distinct models of institutionalization**:

## 2. Comparative Analysis of Institutional Transition Mechanisms

### 2.1 Grandview C-4: Internal Programmatic & Fiscal Dominance

Grandview C-4 exhibits the strongest evidence of complete internal fiscal and programmatic absorption:
1. **Fiscal Capacity**: Backed by a reserve ratio of **93.58%** (\$48.3M), Grandview did not depend on philanthropic cash flow to sustain staff. Career education operational expenditures expanded from \$170,058 in FY19 to \$332,836 in FY24 (+95.7%).
2. **Statutory Board Evaluation**: On December 19, 2024 (MID 16764, Item G.1.c), exactly 12 days before the grant ended, the Board voted unanimously (*Motion by Damon Greene, second by Stacy Wright*) to approve the **Real World Learning Program Evaluation**, codifying student participation metrics and internship pipelines as official district policy.
3. **Curricular Institutionalization**: Grandview embedded the 5 Market Value Asset areas (College Credit, Industry Credentials, Internships, Client Projects, Entrepreneurship) directly into its high school graduation requirements and master schedule.

### 2.2 Center 58: Consortia Contracting & Competitive Grant Chasing

Center 58 pursued a collaborative, regional consortium strategy combined with aggressive replacement grant-seeking:
1. **Fiscal Posture**: With reserves of \$16.0M (23.59% reserve ratio), Center operates near Missouri's recommended minimum operating window (20–25%), preventing large-scale speculative investments in dedicated Career Ed facility construction.
2. **Grant Writing RFP Action (2024-12-16, MID 16561)**: In the final board meeting prior to the December 31, 2024 sunset, Superintendent administration issued a formal **Grant Writing RFP**. This board action is a clear administrative signal that the district recognized philanthropic runway was concluding and sought dedicated external talent to capture alternative federal/state funding.
3. **Consortia Contract Renewals (2025-06-23, MID 18452)**: Rather than bearing the fixed capital and personnel costs of building in-house vocational shops, Center pays annual tuition to regional CTE centers:
   - **Summit Technology Academy (STA)** (Lee's Summit): Two-year extension approved in January 2025 (MID 16754) and renewed in June 2025.
   - **Herndon Career Center** (Raytown): Vocational contract renewed in June 2025.
   - **T & L Welding**: Specialized vocational training contract renewed in June 2025.
4. **Postsecondary Partnerships**: In January 2025 (MID 16754), Center entered a formal Memorandum of Understanding with **GEAR UP**, ensuring continuous college and career counseling personnel for high school students.

### 2.3 Hickman Mills C-1: Capital Asset Dedication & CSIP Accountability

Hickman Mills C-1 established permanence through physical capital and continuous improvement oversight:
1. **The Real-World Learning Center**: Hickman Mills repurposed and dedicated an entire physical educational complex—the **HMC-1 Real-World Learning Center** located at 10301 Hickman Mills Dr. 
2. **Physical Integration with Governance**: In a unique symbolic and operational convergence, the **HMC-1 Board of Education holds its official public meetings inside the Real-World Learning Center**. Governance and career learning occupy the same physical space, embedding workforce education into the daily administrative life of the district.
3. **CSIP Governance Oversight**: In the critical transition window (November 11, 2024 and December 19, 2024 Board Briefs), board oversight focused on **Pillar B: Our Schools** and the statutory **Continuous School Improvement Plan (CSIP)**, treating student career readiness not as an auxiliary grant project, but as core school performance criteria.
4. **Budget Absorption**: In August 2025 (MID 19702), the Board approved the **Revised Preliminary Budget for 2025/2026SY**, absorbing Career Center operational budgets into local tax revenue allocations without program disruption.

## 4. Methodological Implications for Phase 2 Cross-Measure Analytics

1. **Absence of Sunset Motions**: Across all 732 agenda items spanning late 2024 and 2025, there is **zero evidence of board motions to terminate, cut, or mothball Real World Learning initiatives**. Grant termination did not induce program termination.
2. **The Reserve Ratio Moderator**: The financial reserve ratio functions as the primary structural moderator determining the *mode* of institutionalization:
   - High reserves (>90%) $\rightarrow$ Direct programmatic absorption into operating fund (Grandview).
   - Moderate reserves (~25%) $\rightarrow$ Consortium contracting and replacement grant pursuit (Center) or physical capital consolidation (Hickman Mills).
3. **Data Completeness**: With 100% of DESE statutory ASBRs (FY19–FY24), IRS Form 990-PF grant schedules (TY19–TY24), and board governance censuses (FY19–FY26) now fully harvested and verified for all three South Kansas City districts, the empirical data layer is complete.
