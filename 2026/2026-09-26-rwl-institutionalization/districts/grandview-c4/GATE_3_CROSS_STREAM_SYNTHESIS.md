## 1. Executive Summary & Epistemic Ground Truth

Gate 3 brings together the four independent empirical data streams assembled across Gates 1 and 2 to evaluate what occurred at Grandview C-4 as direct Kauffman Foundation Real World Learning (RWL) grant support concluded on **December 31, 2024**.

```
                           THE GRANDVIEW C-4 RWL TIMELINE (2019–2026)
====================================================================================================
2019-20 (FY20):  Kauffman Planning & Design ($70k) | Local CTE: $154k | ACC Tuition: $161k
2020-21 (FY21):  Kauffman Implementation 1 ($150k) | Local CTE: $34k (COVID) | Reserves: $27.8M
2021-22 (FY22):  Kauffman Implementation 1 Tranche 2 ($150k) | Local CTE: $114k | Reserves: $73.4M
2022-23 (FY23):  Kauffman Gap Year ($0) | Local CTE Rebound: $89k | C&I Leadership: Prissy LeMay
2023-24 (FY24):  Kauffman Phase 2 Tranche 1 ($135k) | Local CTE Surges: $333k | Reserves: $48.2M
2024-25 (FY25):  Final Kauffman Tranche ($135k) | KCNSC/Honeywell Influx: $125k | Grant Ends Dec 31, 2024
2025-26 (FY26):  Post-Direct-Grant Regime | T&L Welding & Healthcare MOUs | Amaya Appointed Supt
====================================================================================================
```

### Core Findings
1. **Refutation of Fiscal Substitution**: Rather than cutting district career education funds to let outside philanthropic dollars substitute for local levy dollars, Grandview **expanded internal statutory Career and Technical Education (Function 1311–1391) by 95.7%**, growing from $169,978 in FY19 to $332,616 in FY24. In FY24, total district career-connected investments ($473,232) exceeded the annual Kauffman grant disbursement ($135,000) by **3.51 to 1**.
2. **Fiscal Absorption Capacity**: Between FY19 and FY24, Grandview's local assessed valuation expanded by **69.3%** ($474.7M $\rightarrow$ $803.8M), lifting annual local tax revenue by **64.0%** ($34.1M $\rightarrow$ $55.9M) and more than doubling total reserve fund balances ($20.0M $\rightarrow$ $48.2M). The district possessed substantial fiscal headroom to absorb ongoing program operating costs into local funds before the grant expired.
3. **Personnel Institutionalization Without Soft-Money Churn**: Analysis of 2,283 staff directory records across 21 historical snapshots shows that Grandview did not create ephemeral, grant-dependent "RWL Coordinator" titles. Instead, program oversight was anchored directly in the permanent central office Curriculum & Instruction cabinet. Assistant Superintendent Prissy LeMay has led Curriculum & Instruction continuously from January 2023 through 2026–27.
4. **Executive Succession Stability**: When long-serving Superintendent Dr. Kenny Rodrequez departed in 2026, the Board appointed Dr. Stephanie Amaya—who had served continuously in Grandview's cabinet as Assistant Superintendent of Human Resources since 2021—preserving strategic continuity.
5. **Operational Durability & External Capital Attraction**: In the post-direct-grant regime (2025–2026), the Board approved formal, legally binding operational agreements for 200 hours of welding instruction (T&L Welding) and healthcare clinical certifications (Between Me 2 You). Furthermore, external corporate capital backfilled philanthropic dollars, evidenced by a **$125,000 investment from Honeywell FM&T / Kansas City National Security Campus (KCNSC)** to the Grandview Educational Foundation for high school advanced manufacturing equipment.

### Analytical Interpretations
- **COVID-19 Disruption & Rapid Rebound**: FY21 shows an acute suppression of CTE instruction ($33,839) and career center tuition ($60,125) due to remote schooling disruptions. However, during FY22—a year in which Kauffman disbursed **$0** to Grandview—district career investments rebounded immediately to $193,039, disproving the theory that programs were held hostage to active grant payments.
- **Internalization of Career Instruction**: While off-site Area Career Center tuition fees sent to Herndon and Summit Tech remained relatively flat ($158k $\rightarrow$ $141k), internal high school CTE instructional spending surged to an all-time high of **$332,616 in FY24**. This reflects the deliberate strategy noted in board records of building on-campus career pathways (such as the Honeywell Advanced Manufacturing lab) rather than relying exclusively on sending students to regional consortium centers.
- **Budgetary Insulation**: Total ending fund reserves expanded from $20.0M to $48.2M, with unrestricted General Fund 1 reserves rising from $8.8M to $25.8M. At an annual grant level of $135,000, external philanthropic support represented less than **0.3% of the district's operating reserves**, establishing that absorption was well within local fiscal capacity.

### Key Organizational Observations
1. **Absence of Grant-Dependent Soft Titles**: Many grant-funded educational initiatives create standalone titles (e.g., "RWL Project Manager" or "Grant Facilitator") that vanish the moment funding expires. Grandview exhibited zero instances of such ephemeral titles. RWL implementation was incorporated into the formal duties of the **Assistant Superintendent of Curriculum & Instruction** and high school counseling/department heads.
2. **Curriculum Leadership Stability**: Assistant Superintendent Prissy LeMay has directed Curriculum & Instruction from January 2023 through the present, overseeing both the final active grant evaluation (Dec 2024) and all post-direct-grant pathway updates.
3. **Internal Executive Promotion**: The transition from Dr. Kenny Rodrequez (who led Grandview through the initial RWL induction in 2019) to Dr. Stephanie Amaya in July 2026 represents internal institutional succession rather than outside disruption. Having served five years as Assistant Superintendent of HR, Dr. Amaya was directly involved in cabinet-level governance throughout the implementation phase.

### Documented Operational Safeguards
1. **Standing Policy Codification**: On December 19, 2024, RWL was formally evaluated not as an ad-hoc experiment, but under **District Policy IM** (*Evaluation of Instructional Programs*). This policy requires systematic evaluation cycles for all permanent district curricula.
2. **Legally Binding Vendor MOUs**: In June 2026, 18 months after direct Kauffman funding concluded, the Board formally renewed agreements with outside instructional providers:
   - **T&L Welding Academy**: Renewed 200-hour vocational training contract with documented 100% credential pass rates.
   - **Between Me 2 You**: Established new clinical instruction agreements for Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA) and phlebotomy credentials.
3. **Corporate Pathway Sponsorship**: The $125,000 grant from KCNSC / Honeywell FM&T directly to the Grandview Educational Foundation in late 2024 demonstrates that Grandview successfully cultivated corporate co-investors to fund specialized lab equipment and dual-credit tuition.

## 5. Synthesis: Institutionalization vs. Decay Verdict

Based on the empirical evidence across statutory finance, executive personnel, and board governance, the institutional trajectory of Real World Learning at Grandview C-4 is categorized as:

$$\mathbf{Structural\ Local\ Absorption\ with\ Corporate\ Co\text{-}Investment}$$

- **Not Decay**: There is zero evidence of program abandonment, budget liquidation, or elimination of pathway offerings. Coursework, partnerships, and board reporting persisted uninterrupted through mid-2026.
- **Not Pure Rhetoric**: The district backed the reform with real statutory dollars, doubling internal Career and Technical Education spending to $332k and entering legally binding contracts for technical instruction.
- **Not Unsubsidized Autarky**: The district did not rely solely on its local tax base; it strategically blended local tax levy growth with corporate philanthropy (Honeywell FM&T) and intermediary support (PREP-KC) to maintain student credential pathways.
