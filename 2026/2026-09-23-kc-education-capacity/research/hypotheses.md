# Research Hypotheses: Kansas City Education Capacity Study
## Status & Empirical Adjudication (Phases 1–3C & Tasks 004A–004C)

## Core Framing
**Central Research Question:**
*Is insufficient adult instructional capacity the common constraint behind many of the problems teachers experience—and is class size the best observable proxy for that constraint?*

Rather than cataloguing grievances or designing an analysis to validate prior recollections, this project tests competing hypotheses against empirical administrative data. Every hypothesis has defined observation criteria and explicit falsification conditions.

---

### H1a — Ratio / Measurement Illusion Hypothesis
* **Status:** **STRONGLY SUPPORTED (Tasks 004A & 004A.1)**
* **Hypothesis:** Reported headline pupil/teacher ratios substantially obscure and understate actual class sizes experienced by students and teachers in standard classrooms.
* **Mechanism:** Staffing ratios divide total enrollment by total certified FTE (including interventionists, reading coaches, speech pathologists, and special education teachers with small caseloads). Consequently, an overall 16:1 ratio can easily coexist with general education core sections of 25–30 students without any deliberate data distortion.
* **Empirical Adjudication:** Validated across all 6 CRDC waves. Building-wide high school PTR averages 14.5 to 16.2:1, while observed core math and science sections average 24 to 28+ on large suburban comprehensive high school campuses.

---

### H1b — Longitudinal Growth (Ballooning-Class) Hypothesis
* **Status:** **FALSIFIED / REJECTED (Tasks 004A.1 & 004C)**
* **Hypothesis:** Actual instructional classroom loads have increased over the past 10–15 years, even if reported headline staffing ratios have remained stable.
* **Mechanism:** Shifting resource allocations, expansion of non-classroom specialist positions, or budgetary constraints have expanded core section headcounts over time.
* **Empirical Adjudication:** **REJECTED.** Neither national teacher survey data (NTPS: 2011–12: 24.2; 2015–16: 26.0; 2017–18: 23.3; 2020–21: 21.0) nor Kansas City longitudinal CRDC panels show a secular secular ballooning in section headcounts over the decade. CRDC course averages fell during the pandemic and rebounded to pre-pandemic levels (17.5 to 18.9 regional class-weighted mean). The belief that classes continuously expanded by 5+ students per section is not supported by public records.

---

### H1c — The Allocation Wedge & Schedule Arithmetic Hypothesis
* **Status:** **STRONGLY CONFIRMED & STRUCTURALLY DECOMPOSED (Task 004B)**
* **Hypothesis:** A substantial structural wedge exists between reported pupil/teacher ratios and typical general-education classroom section sizes because federal and state teacher FTE counts include instructional personnel who carry small caseloads, provide specialized services, or do not bear standard core classroom rosters.
* **Mechanism & Schedule Arithmetic:** Decomposed into three additive layers:
  $$\text{Observed Core Size} = \text{Reported School PTR} + \Delta_1 + \Delta_2 + \Delta_3$$
  1. **$\Delta_1$ (Specialist Denominator Effect):** $+2.5$ to $+2.8$ students (removing non-classroom special education co-teachers and reading/EL specialists).
  2. **$\Delta_2$ (Schedule Planning Multiplier):** $+6.5$ to $+8.0$ students ($\text{PTR}_{class} \times [\phi - 1]$; statutory MSIP 250 min prep and 5 of 7 teaching loads produce $\phi = 1.400$).
  3. **$\Delta_3$ (Course Hierarchy / Tracking Residual):** $-1.5$ to $+3.5$ students (allocation between small advanced electives at 8–15 and foundation core classes at 25–28+).
* **Empirical Adjudication:** On large suburban campuses (Shawnee Mission North, Olathe Northwest, Lincoln Prep), **80% to 98% of the raw 10-to-13 student wedge is mathematically explained by specialist categorization ($\Delta_1$) and schedule planning mechanics ($\Delta_2$)**. Furthermore, Shawnee Mission's transition from 6 of 7 to 5 of 7 (approved Jan 2020) structurally required a +20% staffing expansion to maintain class sizes, explaining where the decade's +8.9% teacher FTE hiring went.

### H2 — Complexity Hypothesis (Heterogeneity and Support Demands)
* **Status:** **DECISIVELY VALIDATED (Task 004C)**
* **Hypothesis:** Class sizes themselves have remained relatively stable, but the effective burden of a classroom has escalated because student support needs (IEP/SPED status, Section 504 accommodation plans, English language learners, chronic absenteeism, and coverage duties) have become significantly more heterogeneous and demanding.
* **Mechanism (The Compound Workload Formula):**
  $$\boxed{ \text{Teacher Workload} = \sum_{j=1}^{K} \left[ n_j \times (1 + \omega_{\text{acc}} \cdot \text{AccRate}_j + \omega_{\text{abs}} \cdot \text{AbsRate}_j) \right] + \text{Admin} + \text{Coverage} - \text{Planning} }$$
* **Empirical Adjudication:** 
  1. **Section 504 Accommodations Surged +93.5%:** Grew from 6,552 students (2.03%) in 2015–16 to 12,676 students (3.95%) in 2023–24. On large suburban campuses, 504 plans alone reach 5% to 10% of total enrollment.
  2. **Total Mandated Accommodations Rose to 16.4%:** Combining IDEA (12.46%) and Section 504 (3.95%), roughly 1 in 6 students across the metro carries legally binding individual modifications that regular teachers must document and execute.
  3. **Chronic Absenteeism Tripled:** Surged from 12.90% (37,943 students) in 2017–18 to 35.14% (97,365 students) in 2020–21, introducing severe asynchronous instruction, make-up grading, and truancy tracking friction.
  4. **Coverage Drag:** NCES School Pulse Panel data show 35% of schools operate with vacancies, forcing 40% of teachers outside intended duties and consuming planning periods for substitute coverage.

---

### H3 — Distribution Hypothesis (Averages Concealing the Overcrowded Tail)
* **Status:** **SUPPORTED (Tasks 004A & 004B)**
* **Hypothesis:** District and school-wide average class sizes appear moderate because low-enrollment specialized sections (small-group intervention, self-contained SPED, specialized electives, AP/IB courses) skew the mean downward, while ordinary core foundation courses (e.g., 9th-grade Algebra I, Geometry, Biology) absorb large, overcrowded rosters.
* **Mechanism:** Uneven staffing allocation concentrates unserved seat volume into mandatory core academic sections.
* **Empirical Adjudication:** Confirmed by CRDC curriculum hierarchy. Regional high school Calculus averages 17.0 (with sections down to 4.7), while Geometry and Algebra II average 18.7 to 18.9 regionally and 25 to 31 students on comprehensive high school campuses.

---

### H4 — Null / Counter-Hypothesis
* **Status:** **ADJUDICATED: Falsified on Complexity; Partially Supported on Raw Headcount**
* **Hypothesis:** Neither classroom size nor measurable instructional complexity has deteriorated substantially across the Kansas City region over the last 10–15 years.
* **Mechanism:** Historical recollections of smaller or simpler classrooms represent cognitive availability biases, selective memory, or broader societal stress rather than empirical shifts in school staffing, class rosters, or demographic composition.
* **Adjudication:** The data confirm that raw classroom headcount has **not** exploded (countering H1b), but **instructional and administrative complexity has expanded radically** (refuting the null hypothesis H4). Teachers are not imagining their distress; they are experiencing a sharp increase in cognitive load per student, mandated legal compliance, and operational drag.
