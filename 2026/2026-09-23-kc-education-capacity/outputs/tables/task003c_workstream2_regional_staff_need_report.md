## Executive Summary

While Step 1B-KS decomposed teacher FTE into classroom teachers and specialized teachers within Kansas, Workstream 2 analyzes the broad human-capital allocation of Kansas City's public education system across all **77 fully regional LEAs** over the entire 11-year panel (**2014–15 through 2024–25**).

In accordance with user methodological guardrails:
1. **Parallel Tracking (No Additive Residuals):** Specialized personnel (paraprofessionals, coordinators, counselors) are maintained as parallel series alongside teacher FTE. They are not subtracted from teacher totals, preventing unit conflation and double-counting.
2. **Separation of Non-Teacher Categories:** Non-teacher support roles (instructional coordinators, counselors, administrators) are tracked explicitly outside the teacher denominator. They do not explain teacher FTE growth, but they explain broader organizational capacity and individualized student support.

### Headline Findings:

1. **System-Wide Instructional Adult Growth (+9.42%):**
   - While regional K–12 enrollment was flat to slightly declining ($321,228 \rightarrow 318,883$, $-0.73\%$), the total pool of direct instructional adults (Teachers + Paraprofessionals) expanded from **$26,407.37 \rightarrow 28,895.19$ FTE ($+2,487.82$ FTE, $+9.42\%$)**.
   - Consequently, the student-to-instructional adult ratio dropped from **$12.16 \rightarrow 11.04$ students per adult**.

2. **Paraprofessionals Outpaced Teacher Growth (+11.85%):**
   - Paraprofessionals (primarily special education aides and instructional assistants) expanded from **$4,774.11 \rightarrow 5,340.02$ FTE ($+565.91$ FTE, $+11.85\%$)**.
   - The regional ratio of paraprofessionals to teachers rose from **$0.221 \rightarrow 0.227$** (roughly 1 paraprofessional for every 4.4 teachers).
   - In 2024–25, regional LEAs employed **$16.75$ paraprofessionals per 1,000 students**, up from $14.86$ in 2014–15.

3. **Massive Expansion in Specialized Instructional Support (+49.7%):**
   - **Instructional Coordinators & Supervisors:** Surged from **$536.61 \rightarrow 803.50$ FTE ($+266.89$ FTE, $+49.74\%$)**. The rate per 1,000 students rose from $1.67 \rightarrow 2.52$.
   - **Guidance Counselors:** Grew from **$764.37 \rightarrow 908.99$ FTE ($+144.62$ FTE, $+18.92\%$)**.
   - **School Administrators:** Grew from **$1,175.70 \rightarrow 1,371.10$ FTE ($+195.40$ FTE, $+16.62\%$)**.

## 3. Methodological Implications & Connection to Hypotheses

1. **Rejection of Pure Fabricated Numbers:**
   Both states show substantial parallel increases in both certified teachers and classified paraprofessionals. The expansion of adults inside schools is real across both sides of the state line.
2. **Individualized Needs (H2 Linkage):**
   The rapid expansion of paraprofessionals (+11.85% regional, +15.74% in Missouri) is strong indirect evidence of growing individualized education programs (IEPs), 1-on-1 behavioral support, and intensive special education mandates.
3. **The Non-Teaching Support Surge:**
   Instructional coordinators expanded by nearly 50% regionally (+71% in Kansas). These personnel work on curriculum alignment, teacher coaching, assessment coordination, and compliance rather than rostered classroom teaching.
4. **Transition to Phase 4A:**
   The human-capital investment in schools expanded significantly across teachers, aides, specialists, and coordinators. The next imperative is to examine Section-Level Microdata (Screen 20 in Missouri, KEDS in Kansas) to see how these personnel translate into actual student class rosters.
