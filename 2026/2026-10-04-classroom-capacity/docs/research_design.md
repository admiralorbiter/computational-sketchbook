# Classroom Capacity and Class Size Research Design

## 1. Research Objective

Build an empirical answer to four central questions:

1. **How large are actual U.S. classrooms?**
2. **Have actual class sizes changed over time?**
3. **Has the instructional load placed on a teacher changed even when class size has not?**
4. **What does credible causal research actually tell us about the effects of moving between different class sizes, particularly classes in the 25–35 student range?**

### The Governing Hypothesis
> *Average class size may not have risen dramatically, but the instructional capacity demanded of each teacher may have increased because teachers are serving increasingly heterogeneous students with more individualized instructional obligations. At sufficiently large class sizes, finite teacher attention may become a meaningful constraint.*

This is an **empirical hypothesis**, not an established conclusion.

---

## 2. Four Linked Workstreams

- **Study A — Pure Measurement / Descriptive:** What does the American classroom look like? Estimate class sizes across distributions (<20, 20–24, 25–29, 30–34, 35+) and test weighting sensitivity (teacher/course-weighted vs. student/seat-weighted).
- **Study B — Classroom Instructional Load:** Measure shifts in student heterogeneity (IDEA, 504 plans, English Learners, chronic absenteeism) alongside staffing and class size. Keep dimensions distinct.
- **Study C — Causal Effect of Class Size:** Replicate Project STAR from public experimental data; audit U.S. quasi-experimental evidence (e.g. Hoxby 2000); evaluate non-linearities and threshold effects.
- **Study D — Literature Audit:** Audit the empirical foundation study by study, tracking the exact class size ranges tested in the causal literature.

---

## 3. Geographic Stratification

1. **Primary: United States:** The population from which national policy conclusions are drawn.
2. **Local: Kansas City:** The 9-county metropolitan area (Wyandotte, Johnson, Leavenworth, Miami in KS; Jackson, Clay, Platte, Cass, Ray in MO) as a concrete local case study.
3. **International:** Stratified separately into Anglosphere, other developed systems, and other settings.

---

## 4. Pre-Registered Hypotheses

- **H1 (PTR Divergence):** Actual classroom size is systematically larger than macro pupil-teacher ratio, driven by departmentalized schedules and non-instructional staffing.
- **H2 (Weighting Matters):** Teacher/section-weighted class size is lower than the class size experienced by the average student whenever students are concentrated in large sections.
- **H3 (The Upper Tail Matters):** System-wide averages obscure a substantial tail of students in classrooms of 25, 30, or more.
- **H4 (Class Size Persistence):** Actual class sizes have remained relatively flat even while pupil-teacher ratios declined.
- **H5 (Instructional-Load Intensification):** Individual instructional obligations (IEPs, 504s, EL, absenteeism) have increased over time even if class headcount was stable.
- **H6 (Nonlinearity):** The marginal effect of an additional student may vary across the class size distribution (e.g. 15→16 vs 29→30).
- **H7 (Heterogeneity):** Effects depend on grade level, subject matter, and classroom composition.

---

## 5. Execution Roadmap

- **Phases 0–3 (Initial Scope):** Source registry, CRDC crosswalk, class size panel construction, descriptive analysis of actual classroom size.
- **Phases 4–5:** Instructional-load panel and SASS/NTPS survey validation.
- **Phases 6–8:** STAR experimental replication and literature audit database.
- **Phases 9–10:** Nonlinearity testing and final synthesis.
