# Research Limitations

## 1. Non-Causal Nature of Observational Data
- Regression coefficients and variance explained ($R^2$) represent statistical associations and predictive capacity, never causal effects.
- School poverty composition correlates with unobserved community resources, historical housing patterns, health factors, and accumulated educational opportunities.

## 2. Poverty Measurement and the Community Eligibility Provision (CEP)
- Free and Reduced Price Lunch (FRPL) percentages in schools operating under CEP (or districts with universal CEP) reflect claiming percentages rather than standard income-verified household applications.
- In schools with 100% CEP claiming, FRPL reaches an administrative ceiling (100%), which attenuates variation among high-poverty schools.
- Analysis must distinguish between Direct Certification rates and conventional FRPL, performing sensitivity checks across CEP and non-CEP cohorts.

## 3. MSIP 6 System Evolution (2022–2025)
- 2022 marked the introductory year of MSIP 6 without high-stakes classification.
- In 2022, certain growth and status calculations differed in baseline and weighting from subsequent iterations (2023–2025). Longitudinal trends must account for reporting adjustments.

## 4. Aggregation Level and Ecological Fallacy
- All observations represent building-level aggregate percentages and mean indices (MPI / Mean NCE / Scale Score equivalents).
- School-level associations cannot be directly imputed to individual student trajectories without committing ecological fallacy.

## 5. Statistical Volatility in Small Schools
- Small schools (low enrollment or small tested grade cohorts) experience high sampling variability, particularly in value-added growth measures where a few student outliers can shift school percentiles substantially.
