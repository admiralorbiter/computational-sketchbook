# Phase 6C.2 State Object-Level Mechanism Audit: Vendor Insourcing vs. Additive Layering

## Executive Summary

Gate 6C.2 resolves the core organizational and financial question raised by the macroeconometric null in Gate 6C.1:
**Did school districts—particularly prominent coaching expansions like Shawnee Mission USD 512—expand internal instructional coordinator staffing (`CORSUP`) to terminate and replace costly external vendor and professional service contracts (Object 300 / 6300), or did coordinators represent an additive organizational layer?**

### The Critical Empirical Finding:
**The "Vendor Insourcing / Private Substitution" hypothesis is decisively disproven.**

1. **Shawnee Mission USD 512 did NOT insource private coaching or curriculum vendors:**
   - In 2014–15, prior to its coordinator boom, Shawnee Mission spent a negligible **$1,945** across the entire district on Object 300 (*Purchased Professional & Technical Services*) in Function 2200—amounting to just **$0.09 per pupil** in real 2023 dollars.
   - By 2022–23, as its coordinator workforce expanded from 27.6 FTE to 93.0 FTE (+65.4 FTE), its Object 300 spending did not decline; it **rose** to **$114,986** (**$4.41 per pupil**).
   - Shawnee Mission never had an external contractor apparatus that it terminated. Rather, the district built an **additive coaching overlay** from the ground up, funded primarily through state At-Risk foundation aid ($3.51M) and local general funds ($5.70M), while non-personnel operating budgets were compressed as payroll absorbed **88.8%** of total Function 2200 spending.

2. **Regional Cross-Archetype Mechanisms:**
   - **Olathe USD 233 (Broad Apparatus Expansion):** Rather than substituting, Olathe expanded *both* personnel and non-personnel simultaneously. Coordinator FTE grew by +35.9 FTE, while Object 300 purchased professional services grew from $5.55 to $15.08/pupil, and curriculum software/supplies (Object 600) grew from $11.34 to $32.89/pupil.
   - **Kansas City USD 500 (Dual-Intensity / High Legacy Baseline):** KCKPS is the only system with substantial initial vendor reliance ($244.97/pupil in 2014–15). Its vendor spending contracted between 2015 and 2019 to $100.67/pupil due to fiscal austerity and grant expirations, but its coordinator footprint was *already* dense (>100 FTE) throughout the decade, ruling out a substitution-driven hiring surge.
   - **Lee's Summit R-VII (Lean / Direct School Supervision):** Maintained lean central coordinators (8–12 FTE) while non-personnel support expanded from $115.29 to $185.77/pupil. However, this non-personnel growth was driven entirely by **Object 6400 Supplies & Curriculum Software** (tripling from $39.66 to $124.39/pupil), while Object 6300 purchased services actually fell from $71.85 to $58.19/pupil.
   - **North Kansas City 74 (School Admin Concentration):** Sustained massive purchased professional services (~$340–$354/pupil) throughout the decade while directing administrative personnel growth into school buildings.

## 4. Substantive & Econometric Resolution

### 4.1 Why Did Shawnee Mission's Non-Personnel Share Decline?
In Gate 6C.1, Shawnee Mission stood out because its non-personnel share in federal F-33 dropped from 15.5% to 8.1%, and its real non-personnel support per pupil appeared to compress from $63.51 to $41.59.
The object-level audit explains exactly what occurred:
1. **Zero Initial Outsourcing:** Shawnee Mission was *never* outsourcing instructional coaching or curriculum to commercial vendors in 2014–15 ($1,945 total; 9 cents per pupil). There was literally no vendor expenditure to eliminate.
2. **Dedicated Internal Capacity Building:** Shawnee Mission created its coaching infrastructure in-house, adding 65.4 net coordinator FTE. Total Function 2200 payroll grew by nearly **+$100 per pupil** in real terms (from $264 to $363/pupil in base salaries alone, and over $479/pupil including benefits).
3. **The Non-Personnel Residual is Software and Supplies:** Shawnee Mission's non-personnel support consists overwhelmingly of digital software subscriptions, curricular materials, and instructional hardware (which rose from $3.41 to $30.33/pupil in supplies, and added $537,277 in capital technology outlays). 
4. **Denominative Compression:** The decline in non-personnel *budget share* was a mathematical artifact of payroll expanding much faster than supplies, not a reduction in operating contracts.

### 4.2 Reconciliation with Gate 6C.1 Macroeconometric Null
The state object-level audit perfectly corroborates the macroeconometric panel results from Gate 6C.1:
- In Gate 6C.1, within-district FE models yielded positive, non-significant coefficients on non-personnel support ($\beta = +11.85, p = 0.111$; State$\times$Year FE $\beta = +9.44, p = 0.175$).
- The object audit reveals why: across the region, districts that hired instructional coordinators **did not cut outside contractor budgets**. In fact, Olathe increased Object 300 by +$9.55/pupil, Shawnee Mission increased Object 300 by +$4.32/pupil, and Raytown remained flat. 
- The macroeconometric finding that coordinator hiring was **additive** rather than **substitutive** is confirmed at the micro accounting level.

### 4.3 Policy & Institutional Conclusion
The Kansas City coordinator expansion was not an efficiency-seeking drive to insource functions previously performed by external educational management organizations or consulting firms. It was a structural transformation in district central-office capacity that layered specialized supervisory, coaching, and compliance personnel directly onto existing district operating budgets.
