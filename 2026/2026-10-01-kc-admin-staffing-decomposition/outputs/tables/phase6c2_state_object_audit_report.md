# Phase 6C.2 State Object-Level Mechanism Audit: Vendor Insourcing vs. Additive Layering

## Executive Summary

Gate 6C.2 resolves the core organizational and financial question raised by the macroeconometric null in Gate 6C.1:
**Did school districts—particularly prominent coaching expansions like Shawnee Mission USD 512—expand internal instructional coordinator staffing (`CORSUP`) to terminate and replace costly external vendor and professional service contracts (Object 300 / 6300), or did coordinators represent an additive organizational layer?**

### The Critical Empirical Finding:
**Generalized instructional-support insourcing is not supported as the dominant mechanism behind coordinator expansion. In Shawnee Mission specifically, the observed coordinator expansion is inconsistent with replacing a large pre-existing Function 2200 professional-services budget: pre-expansion Kansas Object 300 expenditures were negligible and increased rather than decreased as coordinator staffing expanded.**

1. **Shawnee Mission USD 512 Within Function 2200 Had No Initial Vendor Apparatus to Insource:**
   - In 2014–15, prior to its coordinator boom, Shawnee Mission spent a negligible **$1,945** across the entire district on Object 300 (*Purchased Professional & Technical Services*) in Function 2200—amounting to just **$0.09 per pupil** in real 2023 dollars.
   - Within Function 2200, Shawnee Mission had essentially no Object 300 professional/technical-services expenditure before the coordinator expansion.
   - By 2022–23, as its coordinator workforce expanded from 27.6 FTE to 93.0 FTE (+65.4 FTE), its Object 300 spending did not decline; it **rose** to **$114,986** (**$4.41 per pupil**).
   - Shawnee Mission did not terminate an external contractor apparatus to create internal positions. Rather, the district built an **additive coaching overlay** from the ground up, funded primarily through state At-Risk foundation aid ($3.51M) and local operating funds ($5.70M), while non-personnel operating budgets were compressed as payroll absorbed **88.8%** of total Function 2200 spending.

2. **Regional Cross-Archetype Mechanisms:**
   - **Olathe USD 233 (Broad Apparatus Expansion):** Rather than substituting, Olathe expanded *both* personnel and non-personnel simultaneously. Coordinator FTE grew by +35.9 FTE, while Object 300 purchased professional services grew from $5.55 to $15.10/pupil, and curriculum software/supplies (Object 600) grew from $11.33 to $32.93/pupil.
   - **Kansas City USD 500 (Dual-Intensity / High Legacy Baseline):** KCKPS is the only system with substantial initial vendor reliance ($244.92/pupil in 2014–15). Its vendor spending contracted between 2015 and 2019 to $100.62/pupil due to fiscal austerity and grant expirations, but its coordinator footprint was *already* dense (>100 FTE) throughout the decade, ruling out a substitution-driven hiring surge.
   - **Lee's Summit R-VII (Lean / Direct School Supervision):** Maintained lean central coordinators (8–12 FTE) while non-personnel support expanded from $115.29 to $185.77/pupil (all objects). However, this non-personnel growth was driven entirely by **Object 6400 Supplies & Curriculum Software** (tripling from $39.66 to $124.39/pupil), while Object 6300 purchased services actually fell from $71.85 to $58.19/pupil.
   - **North Kansas City 74 (School Admin Concentration):** Sustained substantial purchased services throughout the decade while directing administrative personnel growth into school buildings. Note that in Missouri DESE accounting, Object 6300 is "Purchased Services" broadly defined (including audit, technical, operational, and professional services), so sustained expenditures of ~$340–$354/pupil reflect broad district contracting rather than dedicated coaching vendors.
   - **Raytown C-2 (Coaching Overlay):** Maintained a stable coaching overlay (22–23 FTE) with purchased services flat at ~$25/pupil, while non-personnel support expanded through classroom supplies and materials.

## 1. Statutory Accounting Crosswalk: Federal F-33 vs. State Chart of Accounts

Federal NCES F-33 surveys capture only broad aggregates: Total Function 2200 (`E07`), Salaries (`V13`), and Employee Benefits (`V14`). The state-level statutory filings allow complete object-level disaggregation:

| Accounting Category | Kansas (KSDE Form USD-E) | Missouri (DESE ASBR Part III-B) | Federal NCES F-33 Variable | Current vs Capital Scope |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Certificated / Licensed Salaries** | Object 110 (Certified Salaries) | Object 6110 (Certificated Salaries) | Included in `V13` | Current Operating |
| **Non-Certificated / Classified Salaries** | Object 120 (Non-Certified Salaries) | Object 6150 (Non-Certificated Salaries) | Included in `V13` | Current Operating |
| **Employee Benefits** | Object 200 (210 Insurance, 220 SS, 290 Other, KPERS) | Object 6200 (Employee Benefits) | `V14` | Current Operating |
| **Purchased Services** | **Object 300** (Purchased Prof & Tech Services) | **Object 6300** (Purchased Services: broad) | Part of Non-Personnel ($E07 - V13 - V14$) | Current Operating |
| **Other Purchased Services** | Object 400 (Property) & Object 500 (Other: travel, printing) | Included in Object 6300 | Part of Non-Personnel ($E07 - V13 - V14$) | Current Operating |
| **Supplies, Materials & Software** | **Object 600** (640 Books, 650 Tech supplies/software, 680 Misc) | **Object 6400** (Supplies, textbooks, software licenses, testing) | Part of Non-Personnel ($E07 - V13 - V14$) | Current Operating |
| **Capital Outlay & Equipment** | **Object 700** (Property: Equipment & Furnishings) | **Object 6500** (Capital Outlay: instructional equipment) | **Excluded from E07** (reported in F-33 Capital Outlays) | **Capital / Fixed Assets** |
| **Other Objects** | Object 800 (Dues, fees, judgments) | Object 6600 (Other Objects) | Part of Non-Personnel ($E07 - V13 - V14$) | Current Operating |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Current vs. Capital Accounting Distinction:**
> Federal F-33 Function 2200 (`E07`) is strictly a **Current Operating Expenditure** measure. It explicitly excludes capital outlays and equipment acquisitions. 
> To ensure rigorous comparability, Gate 6C.2 provides two distinct state measures:
> 1. **$NP^{\text{all\_objects}}$:** Comprehensive non-personnel total including Object 700 / Object 6500 capital equipment.
> 2. **$NP^{\text{F33-comparable}}$:** Current operating non-personnel expenditures ($300 + 400 + 500 + 600 + 800$ in KS; $6300 + 6400 + 6600$ in MO), strictly excluding capital outlay.

## 5. Substantive & Econometric Resolution

### 5.1 Why Did Shawnee Mission's Non-Personnel Share Decline?
In Gate 6C.1, Shawnee Mission stood out because its non-personnel share in federal F-33 dropped from 15.5% to 8.1%, and its real non-personnel support per pupil appeared to compress from $63.51 to $41.59.
The object-level audit explains exactly what occurred:
1. **Negligible Initial Outsourcing:** Within Function 2200, Shawnee Mission had essentially no Object 300 professional/technical-services expenditure before the coordinator expansion ($1,945 total; 9 cents per pupil in 2014–15). There was no vendor coaching contract to terminate.
2. **Dedicated Internal Capacity Building:** Shawnee Mission created its coaching infrastructure in-house, adding 65.4 net coordinator FTE. Total Function 2200 payroll grew by nearly **+$100 per pupil** in real terms (from $264 to $363/pupil in base salaries alone, and over $479/pupil including benefits).
3. **The Non-Personnel Base is Software and Supplies:** Shawnee Mission's non-personnel support consists overwhelmingly of digital software subscriptions, curricular materials, and instructional hardware (which rose from $3.41 to $30.33/pupil in supplies, and added $537,277 in capital technology outlays). 
4. **Denominative Compression:** The decline in non-personnel *budget share* was a mathematical artifact of payroll expanding much faster than supplies, not a reduction in operating contracts.

### 5.2 Reconciliation with Gate 6C.1 Macroeconometric Null
The state object-level audit perfectly corroborates the macroeconometric panel results from Gate 6C.1:
- In Gate 6C.1, within-district FE models yielded positive, non-significant coefficients on non-personnel support ($\beta = +11.85, p = 0.111$; State$\times$Year FE $\beta = +9.44, p = 0.175$).
- The object audit reveals why: across the region, districts that hired instructional coordinators **did not cut outside contractor budgets**. In fact, Olathe increased Object 300 by +$9.55/pupil, Shawnee Mission increased Object 300 by +$4.32/pupil, and Raytown remained flat. 
- The macroeconometric finding that coordinator hiring was **additive** rather than **substitutive** is confirmed at the micro accounting level.

### 5.3 Methodological Verdict
Generalized instructional-support insourcing is not supported as the dominant mechanism behind coordinator expansion. In Shawnee Mission specifically, the observed coordinator expansion is inconsistent with replacing a large pre-existing Function 2200 professional-services budget: pre-expansion Kansas Object 300 expenditures were negligible and increased rather than decreased as coordinator staffing expanded.
The Kansas City coordinator expansion was a structural transformation in district central-office capacity that layered specialized supervisory, coaching, and compliance personnel directly onto existing district operating budgets.
